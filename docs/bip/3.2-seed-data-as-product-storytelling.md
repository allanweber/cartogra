## Blog Post (~850 words)

### A demo with perfect data shows nothing

My first seed for Cartogra was a SQL file with 200 edges. It was good for performance tests. As a demo, it was useless. Every service had an owner. Every dependency was declared. Nothing was broken, so the product had nothing to find.

A service catalog is valuable when the catalog is wrong. A demo tenant must be wrong in the right places.

### Start from the questions, not the rows

Before I wrote one service name, I wrote down what a visitor must see in the first five minutes:

1. A service that, if it goes down, takes half the company with it.
2. Services that nobody owns.
3. Dependencies that loop back on themselves.
4. A call that production makes but the catalog does not know about.
5. A dependency that the catalog lists but production never makes.

Then I designed a company where each of those is a fact, not an accident.

### Acme Fintech

Acme Fintech is a fake fintech with 21 services and 5 teams: Platform, Growth, Payments, Ledger and Risk. The names are boring on purpose: `payments-service`, `kyc-service`, `fx-service`. A visitor must recognise the shape of their own company, not learn a fantasy world.

Each problem has a believable cause:

- **The god service.** `ledger-service` has 12 dependents. In a real fintech, everything that moves money touches the ledger. Its blast radius is the first thing a visitor clicks.
- **The orphans.** `legacy-batch`, `webhook-relay` and `rewards-calculator` have no team. Every company has a batch job from a team that does not exist any more.
- **The cycles.** The declared calls contain exactly 2 cycles. The traversal must terminate on them, and the demo shows that it does.
- **The drift.** `loyalty-service` declares a call to `fx-service` that it never makes. Three more calls are dormant: `reporting-service → payments-service`, `webhook-relay → fraud-service`, `legacy-batch → notification-service`. One command (`pnpm acme drift on`) switches them on, and Cartogra finds calls that nobody declared.
- **The external call.** `payments-service` calls `stripe-gateway`, which is not in the catalog. It shows in "Unresolved services", not as a fake node.

### Fake company, real signals

The rule that made the seed honest: **the loader never calls `POST /services`.**

The 21 services run as 21 Deployments of one small fake-service image in a Kubernetes namespace. Cartogra discovers them the same way it discovers a customer's services: from the cluster and from the source repos. The fake services emit real OpenTelemetry spans through the collector, so the observed edges come from the real pipeline.

The loader (`pnpm acme load`) adds only what a human adds in real life: teams, owners, tiers and declared dependencies, through the public gateway API. It is idempotent. Two runs leave the same state.

This rule has a second benefit. If discovery breaks, the demo breaks. The seed is a test of the product, not a decoration on top of it.

### One file, many uses

`seed-data.json` is the single source of truth. From it we generate the Kubernetes manifests, the Helm values for the public instance, the 21 source repos with `CODEOWNERS` and `openapi.yaml`, and the expectations for `pnpm acme verify`.

`verify` asserts the story: the god service has 12 dependents, the orphans are on `/risks`, the cycles terminate, the drift appears. Each roadmap story says how it uses Acme: asserted by `verify`, manual, or not seed-testable. A feature that the seed cannot show is a feature that a visitor cannot find.

### What I learned

- **Design the flaws first.** Healthy data is easy to generate. Believable flaws need a reason to exist.
- **Use the real input path.** A seed that writes to the database directly shows a product that does not exist.
- **Keep the story small.** 21 services fit on one screen. 200 do not, and a visitor does not need 200 to understand.

## LinkedIn Post (~330 words)

Most demo data is too healthy. Every service has an owner, every dependency is documented, every chart is green. For a product that finds problems, healthy data is the worst possible demo.

For Cartogra, I designed a fake company backwards. First I wrote the questions that a visitor must answer in five minutes. What breaks if this service goes down? What does nobody own? What does production do that the catalog does not know? Then I designed a company where each answer is a believable fact.

Acme Fintech has 21 services and 5 teams. The ledger has 12 dependents, because in a fintech everything that moves money touches the ledger. Three services have no owner, because every company has an old batch job. One declared dependency is never called. Three undeclared calls are dormant and switch on with one command.

The most important decision was about the input path, not the data. The seed never creates services through the API. The 21 services run as real pods in Kubernetes and emit real OpenTelemetry traces. Cartogra discovers them the way it discovers a customer's stack. The loader adds only what a person adds: teams, owners and declared dependencies.

So the seed is also a test. One JSON file drives the manifests, the repos and an automated `verify` step. If discovery, tracing or drift detection breaks, the demo breaks first.

Three lessons:

- Design the flaws first.
- Use the real input path, not the database.
- Keep it small enough to fit on one screen.

What does your demo data show that a customer's real data would show too?
