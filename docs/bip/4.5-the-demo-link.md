## LinkedIn Post (~300 words)

Cartogra is public. You can try it with one link and no account: cartogra.dev/demo.

The link opens a read-only session in Acme Fintech, a fake fintech company with 21 services and 5 teams. It is not a video or a static mock-up. The services run as real pods in Kubernetes, they send real OpenTelemetry traces, and Cartogra discovers them the way it discovers a customer's stack.

The demo answers three questions that every platform team gets during an incident or a review:

1. **If this service goes down, what breaks?** Click a service on the graph. You see its blast radius: every service that depends on it, directly or not, and the team that owns each one.
2. **Who owns this?** Some Acme services have no owner. The risks page lists them, with the services that too many others depend on.
3. **Does the catalog match production?** Cartogra compares the declared dependencies with the observed traffic. When they disagree, it records a drift with a next action, not an alert.

You can explore everything, and you cannot change anything. Guest sessions are read-only at the gateway, and the backend removes personal data from what a guest receives.

This is the end of Phase 4 of the roadmap: the first version that a stranger can click. Next is API contracts: a CI check that names the consumers that a breaking change affects.

Which of the three questions is the hardest one to answer in your company today?
